04 / THE DETAILS THAT MATTER

Progress you can repeat.

01

The shoulder comes first

For ongoing shoulder pain, especially night pain, difficulty raising the arm or worsening the next morning, arrange a clinical assessment. The full routine is a future template, not a rehabilitation prescription.

Pause painful incline presses, cable flys, overhead presses, lateral raises and pull-ups. Rows, curls, push-ups and machines are not automatically safe substitutes. Use only movements established as suitable; stop if symptoms increase or technique changes to avoid pain.

Maintain comfortable everyday movement. Follow the clinician's specific rehabilitation and pain-monitoring advice. Sudden severe pain, new marked weakness or inability to raise the arm needs prompt assessment.

02

The right weight isn't a bodyweight formula

Choose a load that allows the bottom of the repetition range with controlled technique and 2–3 repetitions left. New exercises: start lighter. Machine stacks cannot be compared across gyms, and old painful working weights are not restart targets.

Keep the same load while adding repetitions. When every working set reaches the top of its range with about two repetitions left, stable technique and no symptom worsening, use the smallest available increment.

For dumbbells, log weight per hand. For assisted machines, more assistance means less resistance. If an increment is too big, keep the weight and consolidate repetitions.

03

Legs need more than extensions

Cover a knee-dominant movement, a hamstring curl and hip extension. This plan includes leg press and leg curls, with glute bridges as an optional hip-extension starting point.

Later, a Romanian deadlift can replace Friday's leg curl once holding the load and performing the hinge are suitable. Keep Monday's curl. Start with two light sets rather than adding heavy hinge work on top.

Core: start with one two-set exercise on A and C. Choose dead bugs, floor crunches or reverse crunches. The abdominal machine is a later option once its shoulder position is suitable. See the core plan below for progression.

04

Food, sleep & consistency

A practical protein target for resistance training is approximately 1.6–2.0 g/kg each day, including rest days. Spread it across meals; whey is a convenience, not a requirement. Medical dietary restrictions take precedence.

For muscle gain with gradual fat reduction, start around maintenance rather than an aggressive bulk or cut. Follow your weight trend, waist and performance over several weeks. More ab work does not selectively remove belly fat.

Aim for at least seven hours of sleep, often seven to nine. Keep A → B → C in sequence when you miss a day. D is optional, not catch-up punishment.

CORE TRAINING

Two sessions to start. A third when recovered.

This is a conservative starting plan, not a universal optimum. Begin with 4 direct core sets per week. If two weeks are comfortable during exercise and the following morning, add B’s optional 2 sets. More exercises are choices, not a reason to do every variation.

A · trunk control
Dead bug · 2 × 6–10 per side.
Keep arms by your sides if reaching is uncomfortable.

C · abdominal strength
Reverse crunch · 2 × 8–12.
Or floor crunch · 2 × 10–15.

B · optional later session
Floor crunch · 2 × 10–15.
Machine crunch can replace it after shoulder assessment and a comfortable machine setup.

Progress: add controlled reps within the range first. Keep about 2–3 reps in reserve and rest 60–90 seconds. On the machine, increase by the smallest step only after both sets reach 15 with stable form and symptoms. For mat exercises, improve control and range before considering resistance. No added weight to start.

Watch for: swinging legs, pulling your neck, holding your breath or using the shoulders to force a rep. Stop or change the movement if it causes joint or back pain. Hanging leg raises, ab wheels, planks and heavy cable crunches remain outside this starting plan because their setups can load the shoulders.

Crunch variations train abdominal strength; dead bugs practise trunk control. Neither selectively removes belly fat. Keep a few repeatable exercises long enough to measure progress.

Why these exercises? Research, practical experience & limitations

Research supports consistent resistance training, progressive loading and sufficient recoverable weekly volume. It does not establish one universal “best exercise” for every person. Around ten weekly sets per muscle is a useful general reference, not a minimum to force during pain or rehabilitation. The reduced plan deliberately does less.

Community discussions often favour stable cable and machine exercises that are easy to repeat and progress. Those are useful practical preferences, not controlled evidence or proof of safety. A popular deep-stretch curl or overhead triceps extension is not a reason to ignore shoulder symptoms.
